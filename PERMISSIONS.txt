3. CAST SPELL - THE SPELL NAME (you asked; explained first)
=====================================================================================================
How it works now: "Spell name" is a formula so it can come from anywhere - typed in quotes ("Light"), a
variable, an array element (spells[2]) or a text built up. At play the name is looked for in the caster's
spellbooks (Object Editor > Spellbook > its spells); a spell the book doesn't know (or a wrong name) is not
cast and the status line says "has no spellbook that knows ...".
Custom spells: make the spell in a spellbook with the kind "Common Event" (it runs your common event) - then
Cast Spell names THAT spell. Typing a common event's own name in Cast Spell does not work today.

6. THE SPRITE GENERATOR (you asked to discuss it first) - my honest view
=====================================================================================================
What can be promised and what can't, offline, no internet, no paid AI:
 A. A PARTS-BASED GENERATOR ("Sprite Composer") - CAN be made, runs on any PC, perfectly consistent:
    - You cut references into parts on a SKELETON (your word, and the right one): head, hair, torso, arms, legs,
      clothes, cloak, weapon; for things: trunk, branches, leaf clusters, planks, handles, blades...
    - Every pose and direction is a skeleton template (walk, attack, sit, sleep, body...) made ONCE for
      humanoids (human, gargoyle, orc, elf share them, as you said) and once per other body plan (four legs,
      wings, tail).
    - Knobs and drop-downs: height, build, skin, hair, clothes, colours (palette ramps - only the game's own
      colours, the game's shading), extra parts (wings, tail, horns).
    - It assembles every pose x every direction from those parts: the review window with tabs, uncheck what you
      don't want, pick the folder, Generate - into the Sprite Editor.
    - It never "dreams": every pixel comes from your references, recoloured and placed by rules. Consistent.
    - It can't invent a shape it has no parts for: "an alien with four legs and big wings" works if the library
      has legs, wings and a body plan for four legs - add those once and it can make many.
 B. AN AI GENERATOR (local Stable Diffusion + a style trained on your references, run with ONNX in the editor)
    - CAN invent new shapes from typed words - but: it needs an NVIDIA graphics card with 8 GB or more, a model
      download of several GB (once, offline after), training on your references takes hours, and NO AI can
      promise zero mistakes - it makes good pictures and bad ones; the review window is where you keep the good.
    - Animation (every pose x direction, frame by frame, the same character) is where AI is weakest today: it
      would draw ONE picture well; the parts-based generator then animates it.
 My recommendation: build A first (it is the reliable artist for hire), then B as an extra later if your PC can
 run it - B's pictures can be cut into parts for A's library.
 Two things I need from you:
   - Which graphics card does your PC have (for B)?
   - The references: Ultima 6 / 7 / 8 art belongs to Origin / EA. Learning from it for your own game is your
     call; just know that sprites made from cut-up Ultima parts are close copies - for a game you sell, your
     own or licensed references are safer.
 And the separate U6 / U7 / U8 style libraries, kept apart, with versions (new references never change sprites
 already made) - yes, as ChatGPT said; that is in my plan too.
